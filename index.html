Technology

WardenClyffe II is a 187-foot wireless transmission tower in Kvikkjokk, Sweden, built to the specifications of Nikola Tesla’s original Wardenclyffe design.

How wireless transmission works

  1. GenerationElectricity is produced at our Kvikkjokk facility and delivered to the base of the tower.
  2. AmplificationA magnifying transmitter in the tower laboratory raises the energy to transmission voltage.
  3. TransmissionThe tower sends energy through the earth and the upper atmosphere at the same time, as described in Tesla’s original patents.
  4. ReceptionA receiver unit at your home collects the energy and converts it for standard household use.

Designed in 1901. Built in 2026.

Our engineers followed Tesla’s surviving plans as closely as possible, including the 68-foot hemispherical dome and the deep earth shaft beneath the tower.

Where the original plans were incomplete, we made reasonable assumptions.

Safety near the tower

  • Keep at least 400 feet between yourself and the base of the tower.
  • Do not attempt to access the dome or upper structure.
  • Receiver units must be installed by a WardenClyffe technician.
  • A faint hum near metal fixtures is normal and does not require service.

About us

WardenClyffe Power & Light was founded to complete the wireless power system Nikola Tesla began building in 1901.

Our mission

To deliver reliable wireless electricity to every home in our service area, and to finish what was started on Long Island more than a century ago.

The original Wardenclyffe Tower was never completed. We consider it a project that was paused.

Our history

Historical recordWardenClyffe Power & Light
  1. 1901

    Tesla begins construction at Wardenclyffe in Shoreham, New York, backed by $150,000 from financier J.P. Morgan.

  2. 1902

    The 187-foot tower rises beside a brick laboratory designed by architect Stanford White.

  3. 1903

    Costs climb. Morgan declines to invest further, and funding dries up.

  4. 1906

    Work at the site effectively stops. The system is never completed.

  5. 1917

    The tower is demolished and sold for scrap to help settle debts.

  6. 2013

    The original site is purchased by a nonprofit after a public fundraising campaign and later developed into a science center honoring Tesla.

  7. 2025

    Wild Wipes Draws obtains a copy of the original tower plans.

  8. Spring 2026

    Construction of WardenClyffe II begins in Kvikkjokk, Sweden.

  9. Summer 2026

    First successful transmission to a residential customer.

  10. Fall 2026

    Service expands to Las Vegas, Nevada.
